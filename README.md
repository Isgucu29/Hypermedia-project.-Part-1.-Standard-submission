# Hypermedia-project.-Part-1.-Standard-submission

CV WEB - ISAAC GUELL CULLELL

This is a one page website with my CV: contact details, profile, skills, work experience and education. I designed it in Figma first and then wrote it in HTML and CSS.


PROJECT ANALYSIS

User profile

The person I imagine visiting my website is Marta Puig, a 34 year old recruiter at a video game studio in Barcelona. She works in the talent team and every week she gets a lot of applications for internships and junior jobs, so she can't spend much time on each one. She usually opens the candidates' pages on her laptop between meetings, but sometimes someone sends her a link and she checks it on her phone.

What she wants to find is simple. First, who I am and what I study. Then the programming languages and tools I know, so she can compare them with the job offer. She also likes to see some real experience, even if it isn't technical, because it says something about how responsible a person is. The studio works in English, so she looks for my languages and level. And at the end she needs an easy way to contact me.

What annoys her is a page that loads slow, has too many animations or hides the basic information in menus. She prefers something that reads like a normal CV. Her goal is to decide in less than a minute if she wants to call me for an interview.

Information architecture

Since it is a single page, the architecture is mostly about the order of the sections. I tried to put first the things Marta needs first, going from who I am, to how to contact me, to what I have done.

At the top there is a small menu with links to each section. I made it grey and small so it helps you move around but doesn't take attention from the content. Below it comes the header, with my name very big, the line saying I am a student at the University of Girona, and my photo. This answers the first question anyone has when they open the page.

After the header I put the contact details. I did it before the rest because if a recruiter has already decided to write to me, they shouldn't have to scroll down to find my email. Then comes the professional profile, which is a short paragraph about my background in programming and web development and what I like to do.

The skills section is next. I split it in four columns (programming, web, design and languages) so you can see all the categories at the same time and not a long list. After that there is the work experience and the education. Both use two columns and the same structure: the title, the place in bold, the dates and then the details. I did it like this so the reader only has to learn how to read one block and then understands all of them. All the sections also share the same heading style with a line under it, so the page is easy to scan.

Visual design

For the style I took inspiration from a black and white CV template that I found and I tried to adapt it to a website instead of a printed page. The main decisions were these.

For the colours I only used black, white and some greys. The text and headings are pure black (#000000), the secondary text like the menu is dark grey (#4A4A4A), the divider lines are #333333 and the space for the photo is a light grey (#D4D4D8). I wanted something serious that is easy to read, and with so few colours the content is what you look at.

The font is Kumbh Sans and I only use two weights, bold for the name and the titles and regular for the rest. I chose it because it is a simple font that reads well on a screen. The hierarchy comes from the size and from using capital letters: the name is 76 px, the section titles are 30 px in capitals, the titles of each entry are 20 px and the body text goes from 15 to 18 px.

About the layout, the content is centered in a column of 1040 px inside a canvas of 1440 px, and I left a lot of white space (56 px) between sections so it doesn't feel crowded. The header has the name on the left and the photo on the right, and the skills, experience and education use columns to take advantage of the width of the screen.

The only decoration is two big black circles cut by the corners of the page, one at the top left and one at the bottom right. I liked them because they give some personality to the page without adding clutter. The contact data also has a black circle next to each item, and every section follows the same pattern of title, line and content.
